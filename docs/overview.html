Portfolio project, NYC

WattFinder ranks EV chargers by total trip time, not by the number on the sticker.

Pick your car and battery level. It scores nearby stations by drive time, modeled wait, and a charge time computed from your car's actual charge curve.

The problem

Apps say a station delivers "150 kW" and leave the math to you. Real cars taper as the battery fills, and each model does it differently. The quoted time can be far off, and you also don't know if the station is free.

A Chevy Bolt on a 150 kW charger averages about 42 kW from 10 to 80%. That is 65 minutes, not 18.

How it works

1Choose car and battery

Model 3, Ioniq 5, or Bolt. Set current and target charge.

2Estimate each station

At every charge level, power is the lower of what the car accepts and what the station gives. Live NYC temperature derates a cold battery.

3Rank by total time

Drive + modeled wait + honest charge time. Open a station to see the curve.

Three cars, three curve shapes

Tesla Model 3 LR: high peak, steep taper Hyundai Ioniq 5 LR: plateau, then a cliff after 76% Chevy Bolt: low and stepped

Anchor points from EVKX, EV-Database, and InsideEVs tests, warm battery. Chart starts at 10%.

What is real and what is modeled

Real
  • 1,656 NYC-area stations, 1,790 connectors (OpenChargeMap snapshot)
  • Published charge curves for 3 cars
  • Live NYC temperature (Open-Meteo)
Modeled
  • Availability and wait: deterministic, time-of-day aware, seeded by station id
  • OpenChargeMap is a registry, not live occupancy
