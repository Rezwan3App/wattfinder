What you built today, in plain English

You went from an empty project to a working app that holds 498 real charging stations. Here is the whole thing as one simple picture. Nothing technical required to follow it.

The one idea to hold onto

Building this is like stocking a store. There is a giant public warehouse of charging stations out there. Today you sent a truck to grab the New York ones, brought them back in boxes, and unpacked them onto labeled shelves in your own stockroom. Your app is the store that will sell from those shelves.

The journey of the data, step by step

1

OpenChargeMap you got the key

A free public warehouse that lists charging stations all over the world.

You registered for a free key that lets us ask it questions. Think of it as a library card for the warehouse.

2

The fetch script I wrote this

A small program that acts like a pickup truck. It drove to the warehouse and grabbed only the New York City stations.

It asked for everything inside a box drawn around NYC, and 500 stations came back.

scripts/fetch-ocm.ts
3

The cache file created automatically

The boxes the truck brought back. A single file on your computer holding all the station info.

We keep this so we never have to bother the warehouse again. It is the reason the app stays fast and reliable.

data/stations-nyc.json
4

Your database on Supabase you made the account

Your own stockroom with labeled shelves. A proper, organized place to store the stations so the app can find any of them instantly.

You created it for free. We built two shelves: one for stations, one for the plug types at each station. It is locked so the public can read it but never mess with it.

5

The seed script I wrote this

The unpacking. It opened the boxes and placed every station neatly onto the right shelf in your stockroom.

Run it once and the whole stockroom is stocked. Run it again anytime to restock from scratch.

scripts/seed.ts
6

The app itself coming next

The storefront customers walk into. A map where a driver sees nearby stations and how long charging will really take.

Not built yet. This is where the green design, the logo, and the map come in.
